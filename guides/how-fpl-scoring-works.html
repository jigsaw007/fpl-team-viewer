How FPL Scoring Works in 2026/27

A practical guide to Fantasy Premier League points: appearance, goals, assists, clean sheets, saves, defensive contributions, bonus points and deductions.

The scoring system in one sentence

FPL rewards what happens on the pitch, but the value of the same action depends on a player’s position. A goal from a defender is worth more than a goal from a forward, while clean-sheet points mainly benefit goalkeepers and defenders. The result is that the best FPL pick is not always the best footballer; it is the player whose role, minutes and match actions translate efficiently into Fantasy points.

DEFCON is the common FPL-community shorthand for defensive contribution points. Defenders count clearances, blocks, interceptions and tackles; midfielders and forwards also include recoveries. The reward is capped at two points per match.

Appearance points are the floor

A player who appears can earn one point for playing up to 60 minutes and two points for reaching 60 minutes, excluding stoppage time. That makes expected minutes one of the first things to check before comparing two players. A cheaper player who regularly completes 80 to 90 minutes can be a stronger Fantasy asset than a more explosive player whose starts are uncertain.

For FPL Peek, this is why minutes and availability sit next to attacking data. A projection without a realistic minutes assumption can look precise while being misleading.

Goals and assists are position-sensitive

The official scoring gives goalkeepers 10 points per goal, defenders 6, midfielders 5 and forwards 4. Assists are worth 3 points for every position. This creates an important trade-off: forwards are closer to goal, but midfielders and defenders can earn more points for the same finish.

When comparing attacking options, look beyond raw goals. Penalty responsibility, set pieces, expected minutes, role and the quality of upcoming opponents all affect the chance of future returns.

Clean sheets, saves and DEFCON

Goalkeepers and defenders earn four points for a clean sheet, while midfielders earn one. Goalkeepers also receive one point for every three saves. Defensive contribution points — often called DEFCON by FPL managers — add another route to returns even when a player does not score or assist.

DEFENDER10 CBIT actions = +2 pts

Clearances + blocks + interceptions + tackles.

MID / FWD12 CBIRT actions = +2 pts

Clearances + blocks + interceptions + recoveries + tackles.

The two points do not stack. A defender with 20 qualifying actions still receives two DEFCON points, not four. This gives centre-backs and ball-winning midfielders another way to build a reliable FPL floor.

Bonus points can change a good score into a great one

After a match, the Bonus Points System ranks performances using Opta statistics. The top three BPS scores normally receive 3, 2 and 1 bonus points, with tie rules applied when players finish level. Bonus is not simply “man of the match”; many positive and negative actions feed into the underlying BPS score.

Bonus can be especially important for premium players, defenders in low-scoring wins and goalkeepers who combine saves with a clean sheet.

Deductions matter too

FPL scoring also includes negative events such as cards, own goals and penalty misses. These are easy to ignore when looking only at recent returns, but they matter when assessing volatility. A player who relies on penalties has extra upside, for example, but a missed penalty can produce a large swing in the opposite direction.

A better way to compare players

Use total points as the outcome, not the whole analysis. Start with expected minutes, then separate repeatable routes to points: goal threat, assist threat, clean-sheet probability, saves or defensive contributions, set-piece responsibility and bonus potential. Finally, compare the next several fixtures rather than one match in isolation.

That is the logic behind FPL Peek’s player comparisons, fixture views and projections: the goal is not to predict a single exact score, but to make the inputs behind a decision visible.
