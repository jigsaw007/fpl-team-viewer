How FPL Peek Team Rating Should Be Read

What a 0–100 Team Rating is designed to communicate, what inputs matter and why the score should support—not replace—your own FPL decisions.

Updated 2 September 20267 min readBy FPL Peek
FPL Peek approach: This guide explains the decision process behind the tools on this site. It is educational analysis, not a guarantee of future FPL points.

A rating is a summary, not a prediction

A single score is useful because it compresses several squad checks into one signal, but it cannot tell the whole story. FPL Peek’s Team Rating is intended to show whether a squad looks structurally healthy for the upcoming period, not to guarantee a specific Gameweek total.

Two teams can receive similar ratings for very different reasons. One may have stronger captaincy but weaker depth; another may be balanced across all 15 players.

What should influence the rating

The most useful inputs are expected minutes, upcoming fixtures, player quality, captaincy options, availability, squad structure and obvious weak spots. Price and ownership can provide context but should not automatically make a player “better”.

A rating should penalise likely non-starters more heavily than a minor fixture downgrade because zero minutes remove almost every route to points.

Why upgrades are suggestions, not instructions

An upgrade radar is best treated as a shortlist generator. A suggested replacement may improve one metric while making another part of the squad worse, or it may require transfers that are not worth the cost.

Always place the suggested move into Planner and examine the next several Gameweeks before using it.

How to compare ratings over time

The most meaningful comparison is your own team today versus your own team after a proposed set of moves. Comparing two managers purely by rating can be misleading because their chip state, free transfers and objectives may differ.

Use the score to find the area that deserves attention; use the detailed pages to make the actual decision.
