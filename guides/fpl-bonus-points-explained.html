FPL Bonus Points Explained: BPS, Ties and What to Watch

Understand the FPL Bonus Points System, how the top three are selected, how ties work and why BPS should be treated as a live indicator rather than guaranteed bonus.

What BPS actually is

The Bonus Points System (BPS) is the performance score FPL uses to decide who receives extra bonus points after each match. It is built from Opta-recorded actions. Goals and assists can help, but so can saves, passing, defensive actions and other events; mistakes and negative actions can reduce a player’s BPS.

The important distinction is that BPS and bonus points are not the same thing. BPS is the ranking score. Bonus points are the 3, 2 and 1 Fantasy points awarded to the leading players after that ranking is resolved.

How the top three receive bonus

In the normal case, first place in BPS receives three bonus points, second receives two and third receives one. Ties change the distribution. If two players tie for first, both receive three and the next eligible player receives one. If players tie for second or third, the official tie rules determine how many players receive the same bonus amount.

This is why a live BPS table can show more than three relevant players near the end of a match. One late goal, save, chance created or card can reshuffle the order.

Why live bonus is provisional

During matches, BPS should be treated as a live estimate. Opta events can be reviewed, and the 2026/27 game also uses a later Gameweek lockdown so post-match data reviews can be reflected before scores become final.

For a live dashboard, the right UX is therefore to label bonus as provisional while fixtures are active and only describe it as final after the Gameweek data has settled.

Which player profiles often benefit

There is no single “bonus type”. In a 1–0 match, a defender or goalkeeper can dominate. In a high-scoring match, an attacker with multiple returns can lead. A midfielder who combines a goal with chance creation can also perform strongly. The match state matters.

For decision-making, do not chase historical bonus in isolation. Ask why a player earned it. Repeatable underlying actions are more useful than a one-off three-bonus haul.

How to use BPS in FPL Peek

The Live Center can use BPS as a match-day signal: who is currently leading, who is close behind and how a late event could change the result. After the match, combine the final bonus with minutes, goals, assists and defensive returns instead of presenting bonus as a separate prediction.

For transfer decisions, season-long bonus can be a useful supporting statistic, but it should sit behind role, minutes, fixtures and involvement.
