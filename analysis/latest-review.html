Latest Gameweek review process

Review pages become most useful when they avoid hindsight theatre. The goal is not to punish a blank or celebrate a haul, but to understand whether the process behind the move was good enough to repeat.

Separate process from outcome

A good transfer can blank. A bad transfer can score. The review should therefore begin by asking whether the reasoning was solid at the time. Did the move solve a real issue? Were the minutes and fixtures supportive? Was the captaincy process clear? This protects you from learning the wrong lesson from a single scoreline.

Review captaincy without hindsight bias

Captaincy reviews are especially vulnerable to hindsight because the doubled score is emotionally loud. Instead of asking only whether the captain returned, ask whether the shortlist and final choice were sensible. If the strongest candidate blanked while the second-best returned, the lesson may be to trust the process rather than to rewrite it around the result.

Check bench and squad structure

Review the bench points calmly. Sometimes bench pain highlights a structural issue or a poor bench order; other times it is simply variance. Likewise, review whether the squad shape still gives you flexibility for the next round of decisions. Good reviews improve the next Gameweek rather than merely narrating the last one.

Carry lessons forward carefully

The review is complete when it produces a small number of actionable lessons. Avoid reacting to everything at once. One or two strong observations about patience, captaincy, bench order or fixture planning are usually more valuable than a dramatic post-mortem.

The aim is to become slightly better next week, not perfectly accurate in retrospect.
