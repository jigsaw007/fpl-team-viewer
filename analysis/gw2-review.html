GW2 review archive: separate process from outcome

A good review article should reduce hindsight bias, not magnify it. This archive page keeps that principle and presents the review routine in a clearer, more visual layout.

Why review old Gameweeks?

Looking back at earlier decisions can reveal whether your process was sound, where hindsight is influencing your judgement, and which lessons are actually worth carrying into the next Gameweek.

Process matters more than outcome

Fantasy scores create a natural temptation to reverse-engineer quality from results. But a good move can blank and a poor move can return. Review articles matter because they train the reader to ask whether the reasoning held up before the ball was kicked.

Review captaincy fairly

Captaincy decisions should be reviewed by the strength of the shortlist and the logic behind the final selection. If the top option blanked while another good option scored, the lesson may be to trust the process rather than to tear it up.

Inspect bench and structure

Bench points can be painful, but the right response is calm analysis. Was the bench order poor? Was the first substitute stronger than expected? Did the squad structure leave you too much cash on the bench or too little flexibility in the XI? Those are useful questions; simple frustration is not.

Take forward only useful lessons

Good review pages finish with a small number of practical takeaways. Too many lessons become noise. One improvement to captaincy logic, squad flexibility or transfer patience is often more valuable than a long list of reactions.

The archive value of this page is that the review discipline itself remains evergreen.

Defenders earn two points when they reach 10 clearances, blocks, interceptions and tackles. Midfielders and forwards need 12, with recoveries also counting. It is especially useful when comparing centre-backs, ball-winning midfielders and players in matches where clean sheets are uncertain.
