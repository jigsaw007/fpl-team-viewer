Current Gameweek preview: build the decision before the deadline

This rolling preview page is designed to stay useful every week. It focuses on the repeatable decision process: identify the real problem in your squad, review the most important levers, and only then commit to a transfer or captain.

Start with the actual squad problem

The quickest way to make a poor move is to begin with a player you want rather than the issue you need to solve. A sensible weekly preview starts by asking what is broken in your own squad. Is there an injury? A minutes concern? A structural problem caused by price or formation? Or are you simply tempted by the previous week’s points?

When the starting point is clear, the transfer conversation becomes more rational. You can then compare different solutions instead of forcing one specific name into the team because social discussion made that move feel urgent.

Prioritise captaincy and transfers

The armband and your free transfers usually drive the largest rank swings. That is why the current Gameweek pages at FPL Peek are organised around those decisions. If your captain shortlist is weak or if your transfer creates an avoidable minutes risk, a good fixture alone will not repair the move.

Use the captaincy page to create a shortlist of reliable options, and use the transfer page to separate problem-solving transfers from emotional reaction moves. If a transfer does not improve the next few Gameweeks or make your squad more robust, saving it can be the more powerful play.

Use fixtures as a planning tool

Fixtures are most useful when they are used across a short horizon rather than as a one-match prediction machine. A strong fixture run can justify an early move if the player also has secure minutes and a role that creates multiple routes to points. A single green fixture is less interesting when the rest of the run is awkward or when the player’s place is not secure.

That is also why the fixture-focus page emphasises swings rather than isolated opponents. Managers who think two or three steps ahead usually spend fewer points chasing last week’s output.

Protect bench quality and flexibility

Bench order, substitute quality and cash structure matter more than they seem. A flexible squad lets you absorb injuries, rotate fringe starters and hold transfers when the best move is patience. A weekly preview should therefore include at least a quick check of your first substitute, likely vice-captain and the ease of making your next move.

Small details such as a playable first bench option or a sensible vice-captain can protect you from avoidable downside. In tight mini-leagues, these low-drama decisions often matter across the season.

Finish with a deadline check

Before confirming the team, run a simple final check: expected starters, captain and vice-captain, bench order, remaining free transfers, and whether any move was made mainly because of fear of missing out. This last step is not glamorous, but it is one of the easiest ways to reduce errors.

The preview is complete when your squad choices can be explained clearly. If you can describe why the move helps your team, you are usually closer to a good decision than when you only know that the player feels popular.
