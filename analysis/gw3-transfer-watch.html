Do not buy last week’s points blindly

Transfer traffic is useful as context, but momentum is not proof. Managers often overreact to the last scoreline, and the most urgent-looking move is not always the most helpful one. A transfer deserves action when it solves a real squad problem or improves the expected strength of the squad over a sensible window.

Saving a transfer can be strength

Many weeks are won by patience rather than action. Rolling a transfer gives you more flexibility, helps avoid hits and leaves you better positioned when more information arrives. The archive lesson here is simple: not moving can still be a move.

Judge a proper window

One fixture can justify a calculated punt, but the stronger case usually comes from a sequence of useful fixtures supported by secure minutes and a credible role. This reduces the risk that the transfer becomes an immediate problem to solve a week later.

Think about the next move too

Every transfer changes the future structure of the squad. Consider the exit route, the next likely price point, and whether the move blocks a future captain or structural improvement. Good transfers are rarely isolated from what comes next.

FPL Peek archive takePublic transfer movement is a temperature check, not a command. Buy when the move improves your team over a meaningful horizon, sell when the problem is clear, and roll when patience creates more optionality.
